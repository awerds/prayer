# Prayer language and accuracy review

Reviewed 17 November 2026. Scope: all 31 devotional entries, their English wording, titles, and explanatory notes. This is an editorial review, not ecclesiastical approval or a fresh translation from critical Latin editions. The Latin subtitles are identifiers, not a bilingual edition.

## Findings and changes

- Familiar prayers: no substantive doctrinal error identified in the Our Father, Hail Mary, Glory Be, Sign of the Cross, Guardian Angel, Angelus, Act of Contrition, Saint Michael, Memorare, Hail Holy Queen, Anima Christi, Spiritual Communion, grace, or Eternal Rest. Traditional English variants are legitimate; spelling, pronouns, punctuation, and Holy Ghost/Holy Spirit need not be identical across editions. The [Compendium, common prayers](https://www.vatican.va/archive/compendium_ccc/documents/archive_2005_compendium-ccc_en.html#APPENDIX) provides English and Latin comparison material for several of these prayers.
- Morning Offering: several forms exist. The current Prayer Network offering is not an exact source for every word of Oravigil’s older form. “Associates” needs its prayer-apostolate context. See the [Prayer Network’s offerings](https://popesprayerusa.net/daily-offering/).
- Angelus: expanded both abbreviated Hail Marys so the visible text and all speech engines receive the whole prayer. Eastertide substitution is stated in the existing note, but the library does not yet supply a separate Regina Caeli entry or switch the daily card automatically.
- Readability: added explanations of older vocabulary, clarified the Latin Catholic shoulder order, and added paragraph breaks in several long Bridget prayers. Traditional prayer wording is otherwise retained.
- Saint Bridget 1: the historical wording about sweat differs from [Luke 22:44](https://bible.usccb.org/bible/luke/22). Added an explicit note instead of silently altering the historical text.
- Saint Bridget 3 and 9: historical language can wrongly imply collective Jewish responsibility. Added visible correction before the prayer in both reader and guided sequence. [Catechism 597–598](https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__P1O.HTM) rejects collective blame. [John 19:23–27](https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0839/_PXR.HTM) identifies the soldiers who crucified Jesus and those standing near the Cross.
- Saint Bridget 5: explained that the language about judgment must be read with [Catechism 1037](https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__P2O.HTM), which rejects predestination to hell.
- Saint Bridget 6: the claim that only Mary offered comfort is devotional emphasis; John also records other women and the beloved disciple. Added a visible note.
- Saint Bridget 7, 11, 13–15: explained figurative language and older vocabulary. Prayer 15’s title summary now reflects the Blood of Christ and conversion; its actual wording does not explicitly describe the pierced side. Its total-blood-loss language is not a physiological claim.
- All Bridget entries: replaced repetitive, sometimes incorrectly lowercased summaries with individual explanations and uses. Preserved traditional attribution without asserting certain authorship. No associated promises or indulgences are guaranteed.
- Prayer Before a Crucifix: clarified traditional Psalm 21 / modern Psalm 22 numbering. Its quoted wording reflects an older translation tradition.

## Limits and remaining editorial work

The 730-day liturgy archive was not reviewed line by line against Latin originals. Its English comes from Divinum Officium and includes mixed translation styles; it is not presented as one uniform Bible translation. Automated checks establish coverage and reader extraction behavior, not theological or translation accuracy. A scholarly review of every lesson and rubric remains outstanding. A verified scan of the Bridget source and a consistent edition-specific source for every devotional variant would strengthen provenance.
